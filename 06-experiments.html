06 / THE EVIDENCE

The experiments

one variable at a time Runs 01 to 03 differ only in the combination rule. Run 04 differs from 03 only in batch size. Same seed, same data order, same optimiser, same schedule throughout.

Two gates had to pass first

Neither gate involves training. Both are cheap, and both would have invalidated everything downstream if skipped.

What each gate proved
GateQuestionResult
reversibilitydoes the rule actually invert? midpoint 9.574e−07 PASS · euler 6.531e−01 FAIL
gradientsdoes the reversible backward give the same gradients? 4.090e−06 relative, 0 of 63 tensors over tolerance

They answer different questions

Exact reconstruction does not guarantee correct gradients — that is a separate claim needing separate evidence. A rule could invert perfectly and still have a backward pass that accumulates gradient into the wrong state.

Dropout must be zero, and in every run

Reversibility recomputes a block to reconstruct its activations. A random dropout mask would be resampled on recomputation, so the reconstruction would silently differ from what the forward pass used.

Setting it to zero only in the reversible runs would have made them incomparable. So no nn.Dropout module is created anywhere — the forward pass draws no random numbers at all.

Proving the data order was actually held

Same seed is not enough. A different data-loader path can produce different batches from the same seed, and the loss curve will not reveal it.

Result 1 — reversibility is free in loss terms

1.768222  vs  1.768609  →  0.000387 apart, 0.022% midpoint_rev against baseline, after 6,104 steps on identical data.

This is what the gradient gate predicted from a single batch, now confirmed over a full run. The reversible backward reconstructs activations instead of storing them and the optimisation outcome is indistinguishable.

The cost is 1.22× per step, and the saving is 51.7% of peak memory at batch 8.

Result 2 — euler trains best and cannot be reversed

Euler finished 0.0194 below baseline — the best of the three — and led at every single validation point.

This does not contradict the gate

Gate 1 showed euler's inverse is wrong. Nothing about that prevents the forward map from being a good integrator.

Reconstruction quality and optimisation quality are independent properties, and these runs separate them cleanly — which is exactly why euler was run to completion rather than dropped after failing the gate.

It remains unusable for a reversible backward. Building one on a 65%-wrong inverse would produce silently incorrect gradients.

Why wall-clock time was thrown away

Median step times across the three runs sit within 27% of each other. Wall clock ranged from 1.47 to 5.70 hours.

The same runs, two ways of measuring

A mean/median near 1.0 means most steps took about the same time. At 5.26 it means a long tail of steps were catastrophically slow — the machine swapping, not the variant.

Measured live

Closing Chrome mid-run took the rate from 10.36 s/step to 0.72 s/step — a 14× speedup, within one validation interval, with no restart.

All throughput figures in this site therefore come from a separate 200-step measurement on a quiet machine, where every mean/median was ~1.00.

Carry this forward: two gates before any training, one variable changed at a time, and median step time rather than wall clock — reversibility cost 1.22× per step and 0.022% of loss.
